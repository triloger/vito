# 2026-11-15 — Project start, policy v1.1

## Context

Account: OKX, **200 USDT**. Instruments of interest: spot, perpetual swaps,
derivatives. Objective: compound the balance under an enforced risk process, with
autonomy granted to the executor once the process is proven.

## Decisions

**1. The policy is the product.** Strategies are disposable; the caps are not.
Every order must pass `risk.size_position()`, which raises on breach.

**2. Autonomy is staged, not granted.** "Autonomous within policy" was chosen, but
the live switch stays off until:

* a backtest beats buy-and-hold on risk-adjusted return (Phase 2), and
* ≥200 paper trades or 30 days of positive paper PnL, and
* 7 days of shadow mode diffed against live fills.

**3. Policy v1.1 — raised from v1.0 at the operator's request.**

| Parameter | v1.0 | v1.1 | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| risk per trade | 1.0% | **1.5%** | The dial that actually changes size |
| conviction tier | — | **2.0%** | BTC only |
| max leverage | 3× | **6×** | Ceiling only; non-binding at 1.5%/1% |
| max gross exposure | 1.5× | **3.0×** | Doubled; gap risk accepted with eyes open |
| daily loss halt | 2% | **3%** | $6 |
| peak drawdown stop | 10% | **15%** | $30, manual restart |
| loss streak | 3 | **4** | Now 24h pause |

Rationale recorded at the time: leverage was raised because it is a non-binding
ceiling, and **risk per trade** was raised because that is what actually scales
position size. Effective position size roughly doubled.

**4. BTC and ETH only.** OKX lists many perps with attractive funding. At $200 the
spread and gap risk on anything thinner is not survivable.

**5. Options excluded until Phase 5.** Contract minimums and spreads make them
unviable at this equity; building the feature before the funding exists would be
theatre.

## Rejected

* **"Grow it by any means possible."** Not a mandate anyone should accept. It
  describes leverage-without-limit, which has a predictable terminal state.
* **Raising gross exposure above 3×.** Three times equity means a 33% adverse gap
  is unrecoverable. That is the floor of prudence, not a preference.
* **Buying a signal group / copy-trading a "guaranteed" bot.** Structurally
  adverse-selection. If the edge were real, it would not be sold for $49/month.

## Open questions

2. Where does the executor run? This machine via systemd, or a $5 VPS later. Not

## Phase 2 — the backtester, and what it refused to tell me

Built an event-driven engine (`vito backtest`) with four properties that make its
output worth reading at all:

* **No lookahead.** Signals are computed on bar *i*'s close and executed at bar
  *i+1*'s open. A test truncates the future and asserts the past signals are
  byte-identical.
* **Stops are resting orders.** Checked intrabar, filled at the stop, filled at
  the open when the bar gaps through it. A gap beats a signal, because the stop
  is already in the market.
* **Same sizing as live.** Every entry calls the same `risk.size_position()` the
  router will call. A backtest cannot take a position the executor would refuse.
* **Costs are paid, not modelled away.** Taker fee both legs, slippage both legs,
  funding every interval while a perpetual is open.

Two years of real OKX candles, 2024‑11‑16 → 2026‑11‑15, 200 USDT start, 5 bps.

### What this says

**1. On a 200 USDT account the dominant risk is trading too much, not picking
badly.** The 4h trend strategy traded 222 times and paid 27.10 in fees — 13.5% of
the starting balance — to finish at +0.45%. The 1d breakout traded 44 times,
paid 2.49, and finished at +8.54%. Nothing about the two signals explains that
gap.
Frequency does. At 1.5% risk per trade (~3 USDT at risk), each round trip costs
about 4% of the amount being risked. A strategy needs real edge per trade just to
break even on the toll booth.

**2. Win rate is a vanity metric.** Mean reversion won 55–62% of trades on both
symbols and lost 25% of the account on both. It wins small and loses large,
which is exactly what the profit factor of 0.45–0.65 says. Any future report that
leads with win rate is hiding the distribution.

**3. The 4h breakout is buying the top of noise.** 292 trades, −38.6%, and only
**8.7% time in market** — it enters and is stopped almost immediately, over and
over. That is not a stop being too tight. It is a channel that is too narrow for
the timeframe: a 20-bar Donchian on 4h fires inside the noise band.

**4. Buy-and-hold was the benchmark, and it was a low bar.** BTC −27.5%, ETH
−41.3% over the window, with drawdowns of 53–68%. In 8 of 9 configurations the
strategy "beat" that. That is not skill, it is an artifact of a bear/choppy
sample. The strategy that must survive the next test is the one that still works
when the benchmark is +100%.

**5. The only result worth defending is 44 trades long.** BTC 1d breakout:
+8.54%, −5.0% drawdown, Sharpe 0.90, PF 1.75. One trade per ~16 days, wide stops,
no churn. It is also **in-sample, on one symbol, with parameters I chose after
looking at the data**. 44 trades is an anecdote with error bars. It is a
hypothesis, not a finding.

### Decision

**The Phase 2 gate is not passed.** The gate said "beats buy-and-hold on
risk-adjusted return"; in-sample that is technically true for one configuration
out of nine, which is what you would expect from chance alone. Live trading
stays off.

Rejected: sweeping the parameter grid to find a better in-sample backtest. With
nine configurations already run, the tenth will look great and mean nothing. The
correct next test is walk-forward — tune on 2024‑11 → 2025‑11, verify on
2025‑11 → 2026‑11, and see whether the 1d breakout survives contact with data it
has never seen.

2. Paper broker (`vito paper`), so the executor is exercised against live prices

3. Only then does the shadow-diff gate mean anything.

## Walk-forward — the in-sample result did not survive

The obvious next question is the one a single backtest cannot answer: *would
choosing parameters from the past have worked in the future?* So I built
`vito walkforward`: train on a rolling window, pick the best configuration there,
score it on the next untouched window, roll forward, and chain the test windows
into one out-of-sample curve.

Two rules keep it honest. Each test fold starts **flat**, so no PnL leaks across
the boundary. And a fold with no configuration clearing the minimum trade count
*sits out* — recorded as a decision, not silently skipped.

### Out-of-sample results

| Symbol | TF | Strategy | Folds (traded) | OOS return | Max DD | Sharpe | Trades | Fees | Buy & hold |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| BTC | 1d | breakout | 4 (2) | **+2.82%** | −1.3% | 1.32 | 10 | 0.47 | −19.74% |
| BTC | 1d | trend | 4 (4) | −2.86% | −14.0% | −0.19 | 14 | 1.09 | −19.74% |
| BTC | 4h | trend | 20 (16) | −7.18% | −28.5% | −0.06 | 141 | 21.67 | −9.79% |
| ETH | 1d | breakout | 4 (4) | +0.03% | −2.2% | 0.03 | 15 | 0.62 | −32.16% |

### What this says

**1. The +8.54% was mostly a fitting artifact.** The same BTC 1d breakout that
looked like Sharpe 0.90 in sample produces +2.82% out of sample on **10 trades**,
and one of the two traded folds carried the whole gain. Ten trades cannot
distinguish edge from luck. The number did not collapse to zero; it collapsed to
"unknown".

**2. The 4h trend is now dead, not unlucky.** Twenty folds and 141 trades is
finally enough sample to say something: −7.18% after 21.67 in fees, Sharpe −0.06,
5 of 20 folds positive. That strategy was never an edge; it was a fee-farming
machine with a positive-looking in-sample tail. The walk-forward took 20 folds to
reveal what one backtest hid.

**3. The parameter search is unstable, which is another name for noise.** For the
BTC 1d breakout, `atr_mult` was picked identically in both traded folds (100%),
but the breakout `window` and `volume_mult` agreed only half the time. A search
that keeps changing its mind is not converging on a structure in the data.

**4. ETH 1d breakout is exactly zero.** +0.03% out of sample. Not negative, not
positive — the strategy has no measurable relationship to ETH's future.

**5. Beating buy-and-hold here is not a signal.** Buy-and-hold lost 10–32% over
these test spans. Everything "beat" it by not being long through a drawdown.
That is risk management, not alpha, and it would vanish in an up-market.

### Decision

**No strategy in the library has demonstrated out-of-sample edge.** That is a
failed gate, not a failed project: the gate did its job before any capital was
at risk, which is the entire reason it exists.

Rejected: running a bigger parameter search until something looks good. The
stability column above is the warning — a wider grid would produce a better
curve and less truth. Rejected too: re-testing on the same two years with a
different seed of ideas. That is how a backtester becomes a random number
generator with extra steps.

### Where that leaves the thesis

Two honest readings:

* **Weak reading (likely):** directional trading of BTC/ETH at 10–40 trades per
  year cannot clear fees plus slippage at 200 USDT. The edge, if any, is smaller
  than the toll.
* **Strong reading:** the premise is wrong, not the parameters. A `$200` account
  trading perpetuals directionally is competing with market makers on their
  terms. The structural edges available to a small account are elsewhere —
  funding/basis carry, cross-sectional momentum across many instruments, or
  simply not losing.

Next, in order: **paper broker** (exercise the executor against live prices with
no capital — this is an engineering step, and needed regardless of edge), then
test a structurally different hypothesis rather than another parameter sweep.
The live switch stays off.

## Phase 2b — the paper broker, and the line between plumbing and edge

Built `vito paper step`: one idempotent evaluation, no daemon.

### Why a step

A long-running bot has to handle reconnects, in-memory state, and the failure mode
where it is silently dead and nobody notices. A step that a timer invokes has one
job, exits, and leaves a state file that either advanced or did not. If cron stops
running, `last_bar_ts` goes stale and the next `vito paper status` shows it. That
failure is visible; a hung websocket is not.

### Rules

* The newest **forming** candle is dropped before anything else. Trading a price
  that has not closed is the live version of lookahead, and it is the single most
  common way a paper bot lies to its author.
* Every bar after the persisted watermark is applied in order: decide on the
  close, fill at the next open, check the stop intrabar, fill at the open when the
  bar gapped through it. Missing three days of timer runs costs nothing.
* Sizing calls the same `risk.size_position()`. A paper position the live router
  would refuse cannot exist.
* The operator's halt file blocks **new** entries while letting stops and exits
  run. A kill switch that also traps you in a position is not a kill switch.
* State is written with `fsync` + `os.replace`, so a crash mid-save cannot
  truncate the track record.

### The invariant worth having

Paper and the backtester are now two implementations of one rule set, which is
two chances to be wrong. So `tests/test_paper.py::test_paper_matches_backtest`
runs both over the same bars and asserts the trade logs match round for round:
entry timestamp, fill price, fees, exit reason. Duplication is survivable only
when it is checked.

### What it does not model

Funding, spread widening, partial fills, and latency. Written into both the
module docstring and `docs/paper.md` so that no future reader mistakes a paper
equity curve for an estimate of returns. Those belong to shadow mode, where the
intended fill is diffed against the real book.

### Status

The plumbing is green: warm-up, no-op on repeat, catch-up equivalence, kill-switch
gating, atomic persistence, and cross-implementation agreement all have tests
(121 total). The strategy question is exactly where it was — **no demonstrated
out-of-sample edge.** Paper will now accumulate a track record against live
prices, which is the only honest way to see whether the executor behaves, but it
will not manufacture an edge that the walk-forward could not find.

Live stays off.

## Phase 3 — methodology audit, OKX TradFi discovery, and cross-asset momentum panel

### 1. Adversarial audit of the walk-forward harness

Before expanding the universe, we audited `walkforward.py` and `engine.py` for
lookahead, selection bias, and accounting bugs:

* **Zero lookahead verified:** `indicators.py`, `library.py`, and bar execution
  ordering (`close[i]` signal → `open[i+1]` fill) are strictly backward-looking.
* **Fixed sat-out fold curve truncation:** `walkforward.py` previously omitted
  sat-out folds from `equity_curve`, shrinking `len(curve)` from 360 to 180 bars
  on BTC 1d breakout and inflating annualized CAGR (~2×) and Sharpe (~1.4×).
  Sat-out folds now append flat segments over their test timestamps.
* **Added Deflated Sharpe (`DSR`) and trade-level `t_stat`:** Re-running BTC 1d
  breakout with the fixed curve yields `Sharpe 0.93 (DSR 0.06, t=+0.78)` — a 94%
  probability that the +2.82% OOS return is selection noise.
* **Fixed `_align_funding`:** Settlements are now aligned to `[ts[i], ts[i+1])`
  via `side="right" - 1` and bounded to the window.
* **Wired `min_notional`** into `run_backtest` and `walk_forward`, and raised CLI
  `--min-train-trades` from 4 to 15.

## Phase 5 — Tier-1 external signals & risk-reducing overlay experiment (`2026-11-18`)

Built `src/vito/signals/` (`FeatureStore`, `RiskOverlay`, `vito signals`, and

### Pre-registered rules (`evaluate_overlay`)

1. **Scheduled macro gate:** FOMC rate decision or US CPI release within the
   next 24h → `block_new_entries = True` (postpones new initiation until the
   next daily close; never forces an exit of an existing holding).
2. **Extreme Greed dampener:** Alternative.me Fear & Greed `>= 90` →
   `risk_scale = 0.25`; `>= 80` → `risk_scale = 0.50`.
3. **Perpetual funding crowding dampener:** Symbol's 8h funding rate in the top
   decile (`>= p90` of its trailing 1-year distribution, min 90 prints, using
   strict `<` comparison so Binance's pinned `0.0100%`/`0.0050%` neutral clamp
   is not misclassified as crowded) → symbol scale `0.50x`.
4. **No bottom-fishing boost:** Fear & Greed `<= 10` leaves `risk_scale = 1.0`.
   `RiskOverlay` clamps all scales to `[0.0, 1.0]` on construction.

### Pre-registered acceptance bar

Promote the overlay to default sizing only if **max drawdown improves by ≥ 2.0
percentage points** AND **CAGR gives up ≤ 3.0 percentage points** across the
4-year window, with the 2026 window agreeing in direction.

### Verdict

**The combined overlay fails the pre-registered promotion gate.**

* On the 4-year span, the combined overlay reduced Max Drawdown by **0.0
  percentage points** (`−15.6%` unchanged) while giving up **−3.59 percentage
  points of CAGR** (`+43.45% → +39.86%`, `−$36.77` ending balance).
* **Ablation finding:** Almost the entire return drag came from **Rule 1 (the
  24h FOMC/CPI entry gate)**, which blocked 9 entries on the 4-year span and 2
  entries in 2026 (`+$1.69%` average 5-day forward return in 2026), causing the
  portfolio to miss the initial breakout candle and enter a day later at worse
  prices. Conversely, **Rule 2 (Fear & Greed `>= 80/90` dampener alone)**
  improved 4-year return (`+315.16% → +325.98%`) and Sharpe (`1.76 → 1.84`), and
  **Rule 3 (Funding crowding `>= p90` alone)** improved 4-year Sharpe (`1.76 →
  1.81`) and shaved `0.4%` off the 2026 drawdown, but neither moved the `−15.6%`
  4-year max drawdown (which occurred during a mid-trend reversal when Fear &
  Greed was below 80).
* **Decision:** Keep `vito signals` (read-only telemetry + hash-chained
  `~/.vito/features.jsonl`) and `vito panel --overlay` (opt-in research flag),
  and **do not** wire the overlay into default `vito shadow` or `PaperPanelBroker`
  sizing. Price trend + ATR trailing stops + Simple Earn remain the live default.

# 2026-11-15 — Project start, policy v1.1

## Context

Account: OKX, **200 USDT**. Instruments of interest: spot, perpetual swaps,
derivatives. Objective: compound the balance under an enforced risk process, with
autonomy granted to the executor once the process is proven.

## Decisions

**1. The policy is the product.** Strategies are disposable; the caps are not.
Every order must pass `risk.size_position()`, which raises on breach.

**2. Autonomy is staged, not granted.** "Autonomous within policy" was chosen, but
the live switch stays off until:

* a backtest beats buy-and-hold on risk-adjusted return (Phase 2), and
* ≥200 paper trades or 30 days of positive paper PnL, and
* 7 days of shadow mode diffed against live fills.

**3. Policy v1.1 — raised from v1.0 at the operator's request.**

| Parameter | v1.0 | v1.1 | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| risk per trade | 1.0% | **1.5%** | The dial that actually changes size |
| conviction tier | — | **2.0%** | BTC only |
| max leverage | 3× | **6×** | Ceiling only; non-binding at 1.5%/1% |
| max gross exposure | 1.5× | **3.0×** | Doubled; gap risk accepted with eyes open |
| daily loss halt | 2% | **3%** | $6 |
| peak drawdown stop | 10% | **15%** | $30, manual restart |
| loss streak | 3 | **4** | Now 24h pause |

Rationale recorded at the time: leverage was raised because it is a non-binding
ceiling, and **risk per trade** was raised because that is what actually scales
position size. Effective position size roughly doubled.

**4. BTC and ETH only.** OKX lists many perps with attractive funding. At $200 the
spread and gap risk on anything thinner is not survivable.

**5. Options excluded until Phase 5.** Contract minimums and spreads make them
unviable at this equity; building the feature before the funding exists would be
theatre.

## Rejected

* **"Grow it by any means possible."** Not a mandate anyone should accept. It
  describes leverage-without-limit, which has a predictable terminal state.
* **Raising gross exposure above 3×.** Three times equity means a 33% adverse gap
  is unrecoverable. That is the floor of prudence, not a preference.
* **Buying a signal group / copy-trading a "guaranteed" bot.** Structurally
  adverse-selection. If the edge were real, it would not be sold for $49/month.

## Open questions

1. Which strategy family first? Candidates: trend-following on 4h BTC (few trades,
   low fee drag), funding-rate carry (market-neutral, small but real), or
   volatility-scaled mean reversion. Backtest all three in Phase 2 and let the
   data choose.
2. Where does the executor run? This machine via systemd, or a $5 VPS later. Not
   decided; irrelevant until Phase 3.

## Infrastructure: egress IP

The development machine sits behind a residential Altice address
(`152.166.28.231` at time of writing), which rotates on DHCP renewal or router
reboot. OKX rejects calls from unlisted addresses, so this is a scheduled outage,
not a hypothetical.

Two-track decision:

* **Now, read-only key:** whitelist the current address. If it drifts, `vito net`
  exits non-zero and `vito doctor` shows the mismatch before OKX does.
* **Before Trade permission:** move execution to a GCP `e2-micro` with a reserved
  static external IP (free tier; static IP free while attached to a running
  instance). Commands in `docs/okx-setup.md`.

Rejected: Cloud NAT behind Cloud Run/Functions (~$32/month, 16% of the account
per month), and running an unrestricted key "just for now".

`config/config.toml` is gitignored because it holds the expected address.

## Deployment

Provisioned a **dedicated GCP project** rather than borrowing an existing one
(isolates bill, IAM, quota, and blast radius). e2-micro in `us-central1`,
30 GB `pd-standard`, reserved static external IP — all within the free tier.

Verified end to end: the VM's egress address equals the reservation, OKX returns
HTTP 200 from it, Vito is installed from the public repo, and `vito net` reports
`match`.

Two findings worth keeping:

* **A new project's default VPC opens SSH (22) and RDP (3389) to `0.0.0.0/0`**
  at low priority. Adding a scoped rule does *not* protect the instance — the
  default rules win for every other source. They have to be deleted.
* **An IAP tunnel rule (`35.235.240.0/20`) plus `roles/iap.tunnelResourceAccessor`
  gives a second way in** that does not depend on the operator's home IP. Worth
  having: the direct rule is scoped to an address that rotates.

Operational consequence: **API keys belong on the VM, not the laptop.** A key
allowlisted for the static address is rejected from anywhere else, including the
machine that created it.

The reserved address is deliberately not recorded in this public repository — it
lives in `config/config.toml`, which is gitignored.

## Phase 2 — the backtester, and what it refused to tell me

Built an event-driven engine (`vito backtest`) with four properties that make its
output worth reading at all:

* **No lookahead.** Signals are computed on bar *i*'s close and executed at bar
  *i+1*'s open. A test truncates the future and asserts the past signals are
  byte-identical.
* **Stops are resting orders.** Checked intrabar, filled at the stop, filled at
  the open when the bar gaps through it. A gap beats a signal, because the stop
  is already in the market.
* **Same sizing as live.** Every entry calls the same `risk.size_position()` the
  router will call. A backtest cannot take a position the executor would refuse.
* **Costs are paid, not modelled away.** Taker fee both legs, slippage both legs,
  funding every interval while a perpetual is open.

### What this says

**1. On a 200 USDT account the dominant risk is trading too much, not picking
badly.** The 4h trend strategy traded 222 times and paid 27.10 in fees — 13.5% of
the starting balance — to finish at +0.45%. The 1d breakout traded 44 times,
paid 2.49, and finished at +8.54%. Nothing about the two signals explains that
gap.
Frequency does. At 1.5% risk per trade (~3 USDT at risk), each round trip costs
about 4% of the amount being risked. A strategy needs real edge per trade just to
break even on the toll booth.

**2. Win rate is a vanity metric.** Mean reversion won 55–62% of trades on both
symbols and lost 25% of the account on both. It wins small and loses large,
which is exactly what the profit factor of 0.45–0.65 says. Any future report that
leads with win rate is hiding the distribution.

**3. The 4h breakout is buying the top of noise.** 292 trades, −38.6%, and only
**8.7% time in market** — it enters and is stopped almost immediately, over and
over. That is not a stop being too tight. It is a channel that is too narrow for
the timeframe: a 20-bar Donchian on 4h fires inside the noise band.

**4. Buy-and-hold was the benchmark, and it was a low bar.** BTC −27.5%, ETH
−41.3% over the window, with drawdowns of 53–68%. In 8 of 9 configurations the
strategy "beat" that. That is not skill, it is an artifact of a bear/choppy
sample. The strategy that must survive the next test is the one that still works
when the benchmark is +100%.

**5. The only result worth defending is 44 trades long.** BTC 1d breakout:
+8.54%, −5.0% drawdown, Sharpe 0.90, PF 1.75. One trade per ~16 days, wide stops,
no churn. It is also **in-sample, on one symbol, with parameters I chose after
looking at the data**. 44 trades is an anecdote with error bars. It is a
hypothesis, not a finding.

### Decision

**The Phase 2 gate is not passed.** The gate said "beats buy-and-hold on
risk-adjusted return"; in-sample that is technically true for one configuration
out of nine, which is what you would expect from chance alone. Live trading
stays off.

Rejected: sweeping the parameter grid to find a better in-sample backtest. With
nine configurations already run, the tenth will look great and mean nothing. The
correct next test is walk-forward — tune on 2024‑11 → 2025‑11, verify on
2025‑11 → 2026‑11, and see whether the 1d breakout survives contact with data it
has never seen.

### Next

1. Paper broker (`vito paper`), so the executor is exercised against live prices
   with no capital.
2. Walk-forward validation of the 1d breakout, and the same test on the 4h trend
   to confirm it is genuinely flat rather than unlucky.
3. Only then does the shadow-diff gate mean anything.

## Honest note to future me

If you are reading this after a losing week and thinking about raising
`risk_per_trade_pct` to "get it back": that is the exact decision this file
exists to prevent. The policy already accounts for losing streaks. Touching
`~/.vito/HALT` and waiting 24 hours costs nothing. Loosening a cap during a
drawdown has a well-documented terminal distribution.
